## 1. System Classification

**System Type:** Conversational Agent with Tool Use / Proactive Co-pilot

**Description:**
A proposal drafting co-pilot embedded in a React editor. It combines reactive assistance (respond to user requests) with proactive behavior (surface gaps, conflicts, and compliance issues automatically). Uses structured tool calls (`propose_edit`, `ask_user`, `set_focus`, `check_regulatory_compliance`, `answer_with_citations`) to drive inline paragraph-level edits in the TipTap/ProseMirror editor and multi-turn walkthrough sessions. Haiku 4.5 handles proactive background analysis; Sonnet handles drafting and complex reasoning. "Good" means the AI surfaces real problems before the user notices them, proposes edits that require zero rework, and resumes interrupted sessions without losing context.

**Critical Failure Modes:**
1. **Ghost edits that autosave** — pending decoration content leaking into `editor.getHTML()` and persisting to the DB via `useAutosave`, corrupting proposal content silently.
2. **Stale walkthrough resume** — `active_task` checkpoint out of sync with document state on reload, causing the AI to reference paragraphs that no longer exist or propose changes to already-accepted content.
3. **Haiku analysis flooding** — proactive gap detection firing on every keystroke without the debounce/cooldown guards, exhausting token budget and adding latency to section saves.
4. **Wrong model routing** — Sonnet called for lightweight analysis tasks (e.g., dismiss notifications, routing decisions), burning 10-20x unnecessary cost per session.
5. **Tool schema mismatch** — `propose_edit` payload not matching the locked schema from 14.1, causing silent no-ops or client-side parse errors in the EditSummaryCard.

## 1b. Domain Context

> Researched by `gsd-domain-researcher`. Grounds the evaluation strategy in domain expert knowledge.

**Industry Vertical:** Pharmaceutical / Clinical Trials (Contract Research Organizations — CROs)

**User Population:** Two overlapping roles at CROs. Primary writers are clinical operations coordinators, proposal managers, and business development staff — they are domain-aware but not regulatory specialists. They draft sections on budget, timeline, staffing, site counts, inclusion/exclusion criteria, and regulatory strategy. Reviewers are senior clinical operations leaders, regulatory affairs officers, and scientific directors who check the output before submission. The AI assists the writers; reviewers are the final human gate.

**Stakes Level:** Critical

**Justification:** A single CRO RFP response represents $500K–$50M+ in contract value. Regulatory language errors (softened eligibility criteria, dropped GCP obligations, misquoted endpoint definitions) can result in automatic disqualification during the sponsor's technical review. Cross-section inconsistencies (budget duration contradicting timeline, site count mismatching staffing plan) are scored as evidence of poor operational understanding and directly reduce award probability. There is no correction window after submission.

**Output Consequence:** The AI's drafted text is acted on by a senior reviewer who approves or edits it, then it is submitted verbatim as a binding contractual and regulatory commitment to a pharmaceutical sponsor. Errors that survive review become part of the legal bid record and, if the bid wins, constrain trial execution.

---

### What Domain Experts Evaluate Against

```
Dimension: Cross-section numerical consistency
Good: Budget duration (e.g., "18-month study") exactly matches the timeline section's total weeks; site count in the staffing plan equals sites referenced in the monitoring section; patient enrollment targets are arithmetically consistent with per-site recruitment rates.
Bad: Budget says 18 months; timeline shows 24 months. Staffing plan mentions 12 sites; site selection section says "up to 15." A sponsor reviewer treating these as red flags for operational incompetence will score the bid down or disqualify it.
Stakes: Critical
Source: CRO proposal evaluation practice — sponsors score "understanding of the study" and cross-functional coherence as a distinct criterion (20–25% of total bid score per industry benchmarks).
```

```
Dimension: Regulatory language precision
Good: Eligibility criteria cite the specific ICH E6(R3) or FDA 21 CFR Part 312 obligation by name and section number; GCP compliance statements use the required normative language ("will ensure," "shall maintain") not hedged alternatives ("aims to," "intends to").
Bad: AI softens "subjects must meet all inclusion criteria" to "subjects should generally meet inclusion criteria." AI omits the IRB/IEC approval obligation from the regulatory timeline. AI uses passive construction that obscures the CRO's regulatory accountability.
Stakes: Critical
Source: ICH E6(R3) Step 4 (2025) — protocol and sponsor/CRO obligations are defined with normative precision; deviation in proposal language implies non-compliance intent. FDA 21 CFR Part 312.52 (transfer of sponsor obligations).
```

```
Dimension: Endpoint definition fidelity
Good: Primary endpoint language in the proposal exactly matches the study synopsis or protocol excerpt provided by the sponsor; statistical analysis plan references the correct endpoint hierarchy (primary, key secondary, exploratory).
Bad: AI paraphrases the primary endpoint, changing "reduction in HbA1c from baseline at week 24" to "improvement in glycemic control over the study period" — a clinically distinct claim that misrepresents what the study measures.
Stakes: Critical
Source: Practitioner knowledge — endpoint paraphrasing is a common AI failure mode; sponsors treat it as evidence the CRO has not read the protocol carefully.
```

```
Dimension: Budget-to-scope traceability
Good: Each budget line item can be traced to a specific deliverable in the project plan (e.g., "Site Initiation Visit x 12 sites @ $X" maps to a milestone in the timeline and a headcount in the staffing plan).
Bad: AI generates a plausible budget section that lists standard CRO cost categories without tying quantities to the scope described elsewhere in the proposal. Sponsor reviewers cannot validate assumptions and will request clarification, signaling a weak bid.
Stakes: High
Source: CRO RFP evaluation frameworks — "basis for costs" is a scored criterion; sponsors weight technical/cost traceability at 30–40% of total score.
```

```
Dimension: Inclusion/exclusion criteria completeness
Good: All sponsor-specified inclusion and exclusion criteria are reproduced verbatim or with explicit CRO-added criteria clearly marked as additions; no criteria are omitted or merged.
Bad: AI condenses five exclusion criteria into a summary sentence, dropping one criterion entirely. The omission is not flagged in the AI's output. A reviewer who does not diff against the original sponsor document misses it.
Stakes: Critical
Source: ICH E6(R3) Section 6 (Protocol content requirements) — eligibility criteria must be explicitly and completely stated; omission creates protocol deviation risk if the bid wins.
```

### Known Failure Modes in This Domain

1. **Endpoint paraphrasing.** AI rewrites the sponsor's endpoint language in more general terms. Clinically, the paraphrase may be defensible; contractually, it misrepresents the study and fails the sponsor's verbatim-match review. Reviewers must compare AI output word-for-word against the source protocol — the AI should present the original, not a restatement.

2. **Regulatory hedge insertion.** General-purpose language models trained on broad corpora default to hedged language ("aims to comply," "will endeavor to meet"). GCP and FDA proposal language requires normative commitments. Hedges read as legal exposure avoidance and can disqualify a bid.

3. **Cross-section number drift.** The AI drafts each section in isolation (or in sequence without re-reading prior sections). A budget section drafted after a timeline section may silently use a different duration. Without an explicit cross-check step, these drift undetected until a senior reviewer catches them — or does not.

4. **Missing-but-implied obligations.** Proposals for Phase II/III trials must address IRB/IEC approval, informed consent process, safety reporting timelines, and data monitoring committee composition even when the RFP does not explicitly ask for them. AI that only responds to explicit prompts omits these, producing a proposal that experienced reviewers flag as incomplete.

## 2. Framework Decision

**Selected Framework:** Direct Anthropic SDK (TypeScript) — `@anthropic-ai/sdk`

**Version:** Current version already installed in `supabase/functions/chat-with-jamo/` (from Phase 14.1)

**Rationale:**
The Supabase Edge Functions runtime (Deno/TypeScript) eliminates all Python-based frameworks outright. The tool schemas (`propose_edit`, `answer_with_citations`, `ask_user`, `set_focus`, `check_regulatory_compliance`) are locked from Phase 14.1 — there is nothing for an orchestration framework to abstract. State management (`active_task`, `pending_actions`, `resolved_items`) already lives in PostgreSQL via Supabase — LangGraph's checkpointing is redundant. The direct SDK is already deployed and proven across all five tool types in 14.1; introducing an orchestration layer mid-project would require re-testing the entire tool-call surface for zero behavioral gain. Anti-pattern avoided: "using LangChain for a well-defined chatbot use case."

**Alternatives Considered:**

| Framework | Ruled Out Because |
|-----------|------------------|
| LangGraph (TypeScript) | Only viable TS alternative with stateful checkpointing, but its Postgres persistence duplicates the `chat_sessions` table already in production. Graph abstraction adds complexity to a tool-call loop that is straightforward to implement directly. |
| LangChain (TypeScript) | Integration overhead without gain; tool schemas are locked; abstraction layer adds p99 latency without providing escape hatches we need. |
| All Python frameworks (CrewAI, LlamaIndex, Haystack, AutoGen, Google ADK) | Deno/TypeScript runtime — Python is not an option. |

**Vendor Lock-In Accepted:** Yes — Anthropic only. Decision made consciously: multi-model flexibility is not a requirement (Haiku 4.5 and Sonnet are the specified models), and the five tool schemas are Claude-specific structured output patterns.

## 3. Framework Quick Reference

> Distilled for the Deno/TypeScript Supabase Edge Function context. All patterns are Deno-compatible.

### Installation


### Core Imports
```typescript
import Anthropic from "@anthropic-ai/sdk";
import { z } from "npm:zod@^3";

// Client initialization — one instance per edge function invocation
const anthropic = new Anthropic({
  apiKey: Deno.env.get("ANTHROPIC_API_KEY")!,
});
```

### Entry Point Pattern (Tool Use with Streaming)
```typescript
// Standard tool-call loop for chat-with-jamo and analyze-proposal-gaps
async function runWithTools(
  messages: Anthropic.MessageParam[],
  tools: Anthropic.Tool[],
  model: "claude-haiku-4-5-20251001" | "claude-sonnet-4-6",
  system: string,
): Promise<Anthropic.Message> {
  const response = await anthropic.messages.create({
    model,
    max_tokens: model.includes("haiku") ? 1024 : 4096,
    system,
    messages,
    tools,
  });

  // If Claude wants to call a tool, handle it and recurse
  if (response.stop_reason === "tool_use") {
    const toolUseBlock = response.content.find(
      (b): b is Anthropic.ToolUseBlock => b.type === "tool_use",
    );
    if (!toolUseBlock) throw new Error("tool_use stop_reason but no tool_use block");

    const toolResult = await dispatchTool(toolUseBlock.name, toolUseBlock.input);

    return runWithTools(
      [
        ...messages,
        { role: "assistant", content: response.content },
        {
          role: "user",
          content: [{
            type: "tool_result",
            tool_use_id: toolUseBlock.id,
            content: JSON.stringify(toolResult),
          }],
        },
      ],
      tools,
      model,
      system,
    );
  }

  return response; // stop_reason === "end_turn"
}
```

### Key Abstractions

| Concept | What It Is | When You Use It |
|---------|-----------|-----------------|
| `MessageParam[]` | The conversation history array passed to every API call | Build up via `[...messages, {role, content}]` — never mutate in place |
| `ToolUseBlock` | A content block with `type: "tool_use"`, `name`, `input`, `id` | Appears in `response.content` when Claude decides to call a tool; `id` must be echoed in `tool_result` |
| `tool_result` | A user-turn content block that returns the tool's output to Claude | Follows the assistant turn containing the `tool_use` block; `tool_use_id` links them |
| `stop_reason` | `"end_turn"` (text response) or `"tool_use"` (wants a tool call) | Gate for the recursion loop — only `end_turn` exits cleanly |
| `system` string | The system prompt passed separately from `messages` | Keep static constraints here (tool schemas, role, output format); keep dynamic context in `messages` |
| `max_tokens` | Hard cap on response length | Haiku: 1024 (analysis output is short); Sonnet: 4096 (drafting produces full paragraphs) |

### Common Pitfalls

1. **Missing `tool_result` turn.** If Claude returns `stop_reason: "tool_use"` and you do not add both the assistant turn (with `ToolUseBlock`) and the user turn (with `tool_result`) before calling the API again, you get a 400 error: "messages must alternate between user and assistant." Always append both turns atomically.

2. **Deno `npm:` specifier for SDK.** In Deno, `import Anthropic from "@anthropic-ai/sdk"` requires either an import map entry (`"@anthropic-ai/sdk": "npm:@anthropic-ai/sdk"`) or a direct `npm:@anthropic-ai/sdk` specifier. Using the bare `@anthropic-ai/sdk` without an import map silently fails in some Deno versions — always verify with `deno check`.

3. **Streaming vs. non-streaming for edge functions.** Streaming (`anthropic.messages.stream()`) is appropriate for the interactive chat path (user sees tokens appear). For `analyze-proposal-gaps` (fire-and-forget background analysis), use the non-streaming `messages.create()` — streaming in a background function that doesn't send an SSE response wastes the connection and complicates error handling.

4. **Tool `input` is untyped `unknown`.** `toolUseBlock.input` is typed as `Record<string, unknown>`. Always parse it through a Zod schema before use — never cast directly. An invalid payload from a Claude error causes runtime crashes if not validated.

5. **Context window exhaustion mid-session.** Long walkthrough sessions accumulate message history. Claude's context window is 200K tokens (Sonnet 4.6), but the Haiku context for `analyze-proposal-gaps` is smaller in practice due to cost. Implement sliding window truncation — keep system prompt + last N turns — before the token count reaches the model limit.

## 4. Implementation Guidance

**Model Configuration:**
- Haiku 4.5 (`claude-haiku-4-5-20251001`): proactive gap/conflict analysis, routing decisions, action queue generation. Max tokens: 1024. Temperature: 0 (deterministic classification output).
- Sonnet 4.6 (`claude-sonnet-4-6`): `propose_edit` drafting, `check_regulatory_compliance`, multi-turn walkthroughs, complex reasoning. Max tokens: 4096. Temperature: 0.3 (some creativity for drafting, but constrained).

**Core Pattern — Tool Call Loop:**

The `chat-with-jamo` function follows a recursive tool-call loop (see Entry Point Pattern above). For `analyze-proposal-gaps`, there are no tool calls — Haiku returns a structured JSON object parsed via Zod:

```typescript
// analyze-proposal-gaps: Haiku returns structured JSON, no tool calls
const response = await anthropic.messages.create({
  model: "claude-haiku-4-5-20251001",
  max_tokens: 1024,
  system: ANALYSIS_SYSTEM_PROMPT, // instructs Haiku to return JSON matching PendingAction[]
  messages: [{ role: "user", content: buildAnalysisPrompt(proposalSections) }],
});

const textBlock = response.content.find((b) => b.type === "text");
const raw = JSON.parse(textBlock?.text ?? "[]");
const pendingActions = z.array(PendingActionSchema).parse(raw); // throws on schema mismatch
```

**Tool Use — Locked Schemas from 14.1:**

Tool schemas are defined in `supabase/functions/chat-with-jamo/tools.ts`. Do not redefine them in 14.2 — import and pass them as-is. The `dispatchTool()` function in `dispatch.ts` routes by `toolUseBlock.name`. For 14.2, extend `dispatchTool()` to handle `active_task` state updates on `set_focus` and `ask_user` without touching the schema definitions.

**State Management:**
- `chat_sessions.active_task` JSONB — walkthrough checkpoint (stage, collected_inputs, pending_paragraph_ids). Read on every `chat-with-jamo` invocation; write after each stage transition.
- `chat_sessions.pending_actions` JSONB array — action queue items (gap/conflict/compliance/missing). Written exclusively by `analyze-proposal-gaps`; read by the frontend to render the action queue.
- `chat_sessions.resolved_items` JSONB — completed action history. Append-only.
- `proposal_chats.tool_data` — per-message tool result persistence (resolutions, payload.changes). Written by the client via `persistToolDataState` after each Accept/Reject.

**Context Window Strategy:**

For `chat-with-jamo` (Sonnet, 200K context): include system prompt + full tool schemas + last 20 message turns + `active_task` JSON + current section content. Drop oldest turns first when approaching 150K tokens. Never drop the system prompt or tool schemas.

> **SUPERSEDED by Phase 14.2.3 (D-1):** the 300-char truncation below starved Haiku of
> context and produced zero findings on placeholder-heavy proposals. As of Phase 14.2.3
> Plan 01, `analyze-proposal-gaps` sends FULL section content up to a 5000-char per-section
> ceiling (truncated tails get a meta end-marker). Treat the paragraph below as historical.

For `analyze-proposal-gaps` (Haiku, cost-sensitive): send section summaries only — first 300 characters of each section's content, not full content. Cross-section conflict detection reads all sections in truncated form. This keeps Haiku input tokens under 8K per analysis call.

## 4b. AI Systems Best Practices

> Cross-cutting patterns for the Deno/TypeScript edge function context.

### Structured Outputs with Zod (TypeScript equivalent of Pydantic)

```typescript
// schema.ts — shared between edge function and frontend via supabase/functions/_shared/
import { z } from "npm:zod@^3";

export const ChangeTypeSchema = z.enum(["modification", "insertion", "deletion"]);

export const ProposeEditChangeSchema = z.object({
  type: ChangeTypeSchema,
  paragraph_id: z.string().uuid(),
  content: z.string().min(1),
  position: z.number().int().nonneg().optional(), // for insertions
});

export const ProposeEditPayloadSchema = z.object({
  section_key: z.string(),
  changes: z.array(ProposeEditChangeSchema).min(1),
});

export const PendingActionSchema = z.object({
  id: z.string().uuid(),
  type: z.enum(["gap", "conflict", "compliance", "missing"]),
  section_key: z.string(),
  title: z.string(),
  description: z.string(),
  priority: z.number().int().min(1).max(4),
  cta_label: z.string(),
  cta_tool: z.enum(["propose_edit", "check_regulatory_compliance", "answer_with_citations"]),
  cta_payload: z.record(z.unknown()),
});

// Usage in dispatch.ts:
const payload = ProposeEditPayloadSchema.safeParse(toolUseBlock.input);
if (!payload.success) {
  // Return structured error to client — do not throw; Claude will see it as a tool_result
  return { error: "schema_mismatch", details: payload.error.issues };
}
```

**Retry logic on validation failure:** If Zod parse fails on an `analyze-proposal-gaps` response, log the raw output and return an empty `pending_actions` array — do not surface the parse error to the user. The action queue will be empty until the next analysis run. For `propose_edit` failures, return the schema error as a `tool_result` so Claude can self-correct on the next turn.

### Async-First Design

Supabase Edge Functions run in Deno's async event loop. Every Anthropic SDK call is async. Key rules:

- **Never block the event loop.** All DB reads (Supabase client) and API calls (Anthropic) are `await`-ed, never synchronous.
- **`analyze-proposal-gaps` is fire-and-forget from the client.** The frontend sends the request and does not await the response in the render path. The function writes to `chat_sessions.pending_actions` on completion; the frontend subscribes to DB changes via Supabase Realtime.
- **Streaming for interactive chat only.** Use `anthropic.messages.stream()` + SSE response for `chat-with-jamo` to give users token-by-token feedback. Use `messages.create()` (non-streaming) for `analyze-proposal-gaps`.
- **One common mistake:** Wrapping `await anthropic.messages.create()` in a `try/catch` without re-throwing means API errors (rate limits, context overflow) are silently swallowed. Always re-throw after logging.

### Prompt Engineering Discipline

- **System prompt = invariants.** Role, output format constraints, tool schemas summary, and "never do X" rules live in the system prompt. This content is static and cache-able.
- **User turn = dynamic context.** Proposal content, `active_task` state, current section key, and the user's message are injected as the last user turn. Regenerated on every call — not cached.
- **Prompt caching.** The Anthropic API supports prompt caching via `cache_control: { type: "ephemeral" }` on message content blocks. Mark the system prompt and tool schema block with cache control to reduce input token cost on repeat calls (same proposal, multiple turns). Cache TTL is 5 minutes — appropriate for walkthrough sessions.
- **Regulatory constraint prompt.** Add to the system prompt for any section tagged `regulatory_strategy`, `eligibility`, or `protocol_compliance`: "Use normative GCP language (will, shall, must). Do not hedge obligations with aims to, endeavors to, or intends to. Reproduce sponsor-provided endpoint language verbatim — do not paraphrase."
- **Few-shot for gap detection.** The Haiku analysis prompt benefits from 2-3 examples of genuine gaps vs. intentionally thin sections. Include as few-shot examples in the system prompt, not the user turn.

### Context Window Management

**chat-with-jamo (Sonnet, 200K):**
```typescript
function buildSlidingWindow(
  messages: Anthropic.MessageParam[],
  maxTurns = 20,
): Anthropic.MessageParam[] {
  // Always keep tool_result pairs together — never split a tool_use / tool_result pair
  const trimmed = messages.slice(-maxTurns * 2); // rough cut
  // Verify the first message is a user turn (not a dangling assistant turn)
  const firstUserIdx = trimmed.findIndex((m) => m.role === "user");
  return firstUserIdx > 0 ? trimmed.slice(firstUserIdx) : trimmed;
}
```

**analyze-proposal-gaps (Haiku, cost-sensitive):**
Send section summaries only — `content.slice(0, 300)` per section. Cross-section conflict detection needs all sections but truncated. Do not send full section HTML — strip tags first.

| `chat-with-jamo` (ask_user / set_focus) | Haiku 4.5 | 8K–12K | 256 | ~$0.005 | 2 seconds |

**Budget guard:** If a single session exceeds 50 Haiku calls or 10 Sonnet calls, log a warning and surface it in the flywheel metrics. These thresholds indicate a runaway loop or missing debounce guard.

## 5. Evaluation Strategy

### Dimensions

Each dimension is grounded in Section 1 (critical failure modes) or Section 1b (domain rubric ingredients). Generic dimensions are used only where no domain-specific rubric ingredient applies.

| Dimension | Pass Criterion | Fail Criterion | Measurement | Priority |
|-----------|---------------|----------------|-------------|----------|
| **Decoration isolation** (ghost edit safety) | `editor.getHTML()` contains zero ghost paragraph content while pending edits exist; autosave payload is unchanged from pre-ghost state | Any ghost text appears in `editor.getHTML()` output, even partially; autosave writes ghost content to `proposal_sections.content` | Code | Critical |
| **Tool schema compliance** (`propose_edit`) | Every `propose_edit` tool call payload parses against the 14.1-locked schema without error; all required fields (`section_key`, `changes[]`, each change's `type`, `paragraph_id`, `content`) present and correctly typed | Payload missing any required field, wrong type on `paragraph_id`, or `changes` array empty when edits were intended; `EditSummaryCard` receives a parse error | Code | Critical |
| **Model routing accuracy** | Haiku 4.5 is called for: gap analysis, conflict detection, routing decisions, action queue generation. Sonnet is called for: `propose_edit` drafting, `check_regulatory_compliance`, multi-turn walkthroughs. No Sonnet call for tasks classified as analysis-only | Sonnet invoked for any Haiku-designated task; model parameter on edge function call does not match routing rule for the request type | Code | Critical |
| **Walkthrough resume correctness** | On page reload with a non-null, non-complete `active_task`: resume banner renders; "Continue" re-presents the last unanswered `ask_user` card or unresolved `EditSummaryCard`; `stage` and `collected_inputs` match pre-reload state | Resume banner absent when `active_task.stage` is `gathering_inputs` or `drafting`; "Continue" triggers a fresh walkthrough from stage 0 losing collected inputs; `pending_paragraph_ids` references IDs that no longer exist without triggering the staleness path | Code + Human | Critical |
| **Haiku call rate (flooding prevention)** | Gap analysis fires at most once per section save event, with a minimum 3-second debounce; total Haiku calls per user session do not exceed 1 call per 3 seconds across all sections | Gap analysis fires on individual keystrokes (before debounce window); more than 1 Haiku call triggered within a 3-second window for the same section; token budget alert threshold reached within a single session | Code | Critical |
| **Staleness detection** | When a user edits or deletes the anchor paragraph of a pending modification or deletion, `auto_rejected_stale` fires: the ghost decoration is removed, the `EditSummaryCard` tally reflects the auto-rejection, and no Accept action is possible for that change | Ghost decoration persists after its anchor paragraph is deleted; user can Accept a stale change whose anchor no longer exists; summary card tally does not update on auto-rejection | Code | Critical |
| **Regulatory language precision** | AI-proposed text for compliance sections uses normative GCP verbs ("will ensure," "shall maintain," "must"); no hedged alternatives ("aims to," "intends to," "endeavors to") appear in draft output; ICH E6(R3) and FDA 21 CFR Part 312 references use correct section numbers when cited | Any proposed paragraph containing "aims to comply," "will endeavor," "intends to meet," or equivalent hedges in place of normative obligation language; a regulatory citation with an incorrect section number | LLM Judge (calibrated against Regulatory Affairs Officer) | Critical |
| **Endpoint definition fidelity** | When a sponsor protocol excerpt is present in context, `propose_edit` output for endpoint sections reproduces the primary endpoint language verbatim or flags any deviation explicitly; paraphrasing that changes clinical meaning is absent | AI output changes "reduction in HbA1c from baseline at week 24" to "improvement in glycemic control" or any equivalent clinical paraphrase; endpoint hierarchy (primary / key secondary / exploratory) is reordered or collapsed | LLM Judge (calibrated against Senior Reviewer) | Critical |
| **Cross-section numerical consistency** | Numbers proposed by the AI across sections are internally consistent: budget duration matches timeline total weeks; site count in staffing plan equals sites in monitoring section; enrollment targets are arithmetically consistent with per-site rates | Proposed text introduces a duration, site count, or enrollment number that contradicts a value already present in another section; conflict detection does not surface the discrepancy in `pending_actions` | LLM Judge + Code (arithmetic cross-check) | Critical |
| **Inclusion/exclusion criteria completeness** | When a sponsor document is in context containing N inclusion and M exclusion criteria, the AI's proposed eligibility section contains all N + M criteria without condensing, merging, or omitting any; CRO-added criteria are explicitly marked as additions | Any sponsor-specified criterion is absent from the proposed text; two distinct criteria are merged into a single sentence; an omission is not flagged in the AI's output or action queue | LLM Judge (calibrated against Regulatory Affairs Officer) | Critical |
| **Gap detection precision** | Haiku-generated action queue items for a proposal section with genuine gaps (thin content, missing IRB timeline, no DMC reference) include all real gaps; false positive rate — items flagged on intentionally thin sections awaiting sponsor clarification — is below 20% | Action queue surfaces zero items for a section with verifiable gaps; more than 1 in 5 flagged items are false positives on intentionally placeholder sections as judged by a Proposal Manager | LLM Judge + Human (Proposal Manager calibration) | High |
| **Walkthrough goal completion** | A walkthrough initiated from the action queue reaches `propose_edit` via the `ask_user` → answer → (optional follow-up) → draft path; the AI asks at least 2 clarifying questions before drafting; the draft renders as inline ghosts in the editor | Walkthrough ends before calling `propose_edit` (AI stops at `ask_user` without proceeding); fewer than 2 `ask_user` calls before drafting; `propose_edit` result does not trigger ghost decoration materialization in the editor | Code + Human | High |
| **Budget-to-scope traceability** | Budget section draft includes explicit quantity-to-deliverable linkages (e.g., "Site Initiation Visit x 12 sites" references the timeline milestone and staffing headcount); no line item is a floating cost category without a scope anchor | AI proposes a budget section listing standard CRO cost categories (monitoring, data management, regulatory) without tying quantities to any value in the timeline or staffing sections | LLM Judge (calibrated against Senior Clinical Operations Director) | High |
| **Instruction following / task completion** | When a user requests a specific tool call action (e.g., "check regulatory compliance on the eligibility section"), the correct tool is called with the correct `section_key`; the action completes end-to-end without the AI abandoning mid-turn | AI responds with a text explanation instead of calling the requested tool; tool is called with the wrong section key; walkthrough enters an unrecoverable state requiring a page reload to exit | Code + Human | High |

### Rubric Detail: LLM Judge Calibration

LLM judges are used for three dimensions (regulatory language precision, endpoint fidelity, criteria completeness) where Code cannot reliably detect semantic failures. Before trusting LLM judge scores in CI, calibrate each judge rubric against human scores from the designated domain expert role. Target Pearson correlation >= 0.7 before the judge is used as a gate.

**Judge prompt template (regulatory language precision):**
```
You are a regulatory affairs expert reviewing AI-drafted text for a CRO proposal.
Score the following proposed paragraph on a scale of 1–5:
5 = All obligation language uses normative GCP verbs (will, shall, must); no hedging; any regulatory citations are correct.
3 = Mostly normative language; one instance of hedging that a reviewer would flag but not disqualify.
1 = Hedged language present ("aims to," "endeavors to," "intends to") in place of a normative obligation; or a regulatory citation is incorrect.
Proposed text: {proposed_text}
Source obligation context: {source_context}
```

Calibration: Present 20 labeled examples (10 pass, 10 fail with varying severity) to a Regulatory Affairs Officer; compare their scores to the LLM judge's scores. Adjust the prompt if correlation is below 0.7.

## 6. Guardrails

### Online (Real-Time)

These run synchronously on every request. Each adds latency — kept to the minimum set for which a failure would be catastrophic.

| Guardrail | Trigger | Intervention | Rationale |
|-----------|---------|--------------|-----------|
| **Ghost isolation check** | Every `propose_edit` tool result processed by the client | Before dispatching `SET_PENDING_EDITS`, assert `editor.getHTML()` does not contain any string from `changes[].content`; if it does, block dispatch and log error with `proposal_id` + `section_key` | Ghost content autosaving is silent and irreversible — the single highest-severity failure mode |
| **Tool schema validation** | Every tool call response received from the edge function | Parse `propose_edit` payload against the locked 14.1 Zod schema client-side before rendering `EditSummaryCard`; on parse failure, show error card, do not dispatch `SET_PENDING_EDITS`, log schema diff | Silent no-ops and corrupted summary cards are indistinguishable from success without this gate |
| **Regulatory hedge detection** | Every `propose_edit` or `check_regulatory_compliance` response for sections tagged `regulatory_strategy`, `eligibility`, or `protocol_compliance` | Scan proposed text for hedge phrases ("aims to", "endeavors to", "intends to", "will try to", "seeks to ensure"); if found, prepend a visible warning banner to the `EditSummaryCard`: "Review: proposed text may use non-normative language — verify against GCP obligations before accepting" | Hedged regulatory language can disqualify a bid; writers are not regulatory specialists and will not catch this without a prompt |
| **Haiku rate limiter** | Every invocation of `analyze-proposal-gaps` edge function | Check timestamp of last call for the same `(proposal_id, section_key)` pair in `chat_sessions`; reject calls within a 3-second window with HTTP 429; client does not retry on 429 | Flooding prevention for Haiku analysis — prevents token budget exhaustion and save-response latency spikes |

### Offline (Flywheel)

These run on sampled batches and feed improvement of prompts, routing rules, and the reference dataset.

| Metric | Sampling Strategy | Action on Degradation |
|--------|------------------|-----------------------|
| **Regulatory hedge rate** | 100% of accepted `propose_edit` changes in `regulatory_strategy`, `eligibility`, `protocol_compliance` sections — LLM judge scores against the calibrated rubric nightly | If hedge rate exceeds 5% of accepted changes in a 7-day window, review system prompt for hedging suppression; add failing examples to reference dataset |
| **Endpoint paraphrase rate** | 100% of `propose_edit` changes where a sponsor protocol excerpt is in the session context — Senior Reviewer spot-checks 10% of flagged cases weekly | If paraphrase rate exceeds 3% of changes touching endpoint sections, update prompt with verbatim-reproduction constraint; alert engineering lead |
| **Gap detection false positive rate** | Weekly batch: all `pending_actions` items dismissed by the user within 60 seconds of appearing (proxy for "this wasn't a real gap") | If false positive proxy rate exceeds 20%, route 20 dismissed items to Proposal Manager for manual classification; adjust Haiku analysis prompt if confirmed false positives cluster around a specific section type |
| **Walkthrough completion rate** | All sessions where `active_task.stage` transitions to `gathering_inputs` — track rate of reaching `drafting` then `complete` vs. abandonment | If completion rate drops below 60%, sample 10 abandoned walkthroughs for manual review; identify where the AI stalls (stuck in `ask_user` loop, never calls `propose_edit`) |
| **Model routing accuracy** | 5% random sample of all edge function calls — log `model` parameter vs. expected routing rule | If any Sonnet call is detected for a Haiku-designated task type, treat as P1 routing bug; fix immediately, do not wait for next flywheel cycle |
| **Resume fidelity** | All sessions where `active_task` is non-null on panel mount — track whether user clicks "Continue" vs. "Start over" | If "Start over" rate exceeds 30%, inspect whether `active_task` state is diverging from actual editor state; may indicate anchor paragraph staleness not being caught at resume time |

**Alert Thresholds:**

| Alert | Threshold | Severity | Response |
|-------|-----------|----------|----------|
| Ghost isolation assertion failure | Any single occurrence | P0 — page | Disable `SET_PENDING_EDITS` dispatch via feature flag; investigate immediately; ghost content may be in DB |
| `propose_edit` schema parse failure rate | > 1% of tool results in any 1-hour window | P1 | Check edge function deployment for schema regression; roll back if confirmed |
| Sonnet call for Haiku-designated task | Any occurrence in production | P1 | Routing bug — fix same day; log full request context for diagnosis |
| Haiku calls per session | > 20 calls in a single session | P2 | Debounce may have broken; check `analyze-proposal-gaps` invocation path |
| Regulatory hedge banner shown rate | > 10% of `propose_edit` results in regulatory sections over 24 hours | P2 | System prompt hedging suppression may be regressing; review prompt and add failing case to reference dataset |
| Walkthrough completion rate | < 60% over 7-day rolling window | P2 | Review abandoned walkthroughs; likely stall in `ask_user` loop |
| Edge function p95 latency (Haiku) | > 5 seconds | P3 | Investigate whether context payload size has grown; check cross-section summary truncation |

---

**Smart Sampling Strategy:**

Weight sampling toward interactions with higher failure signal. Do not sample uniformly.

| Signal | Sampling Weight | Rationale |
|--------|----------------|-----------|
| Session contains a regulatory section edit | 100% (always sample) | Highest-stakes output; regulatory language errors are bid-disqualifying |
| Walkthrough reached `drafting` stage | 100% | Drafting sessions produce the most consequential AI text |
| Ghost isolation assertion fired | 100% | Critical incident — no sampling, full review |
| User clicked "Start over" on resume banner | 100% | Indicates resume fidelity failure; needs manual inspection |
| Regulatory hedge banner was shown | 100% | AI produced non-normative language; verify the proposed text |
| User dismissed an action queue item within 60 seconds | 50% | False positive proxy — needs classification |
| Session with no tool calls (pure text response) | 5% | Low signal; sample for safety and tone baseline only |
| Standard session, no signals above | 2% | Background quality baseline |

Sampling reviews are routed to the designated domain expert role based on section type (Regulatory Affairs Officer for compliance sections, Senior Clinical Operations Director for budget/timeline/staffing, Senior Reviewer for endpoint and criteria sections).
